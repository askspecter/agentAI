Documentation

A complete guide to how Olea works, end to end.

Overview

Olea is an autonomous agent on the Robinhood network that turns everyday card spending into a self-sustaining investment flywheel. You keep spending exactly as you do today. Behind the scenes, Olea rounds up each purchase, invests the spare change into a basket of tokenized stocks on Robinhood Chain, earns DeFi yield on the idle reserve, and uses that yield to quietly pay down your card, so your invested principal keeps compounding.

The whole thing is designed to be hands-off but never unchecked: the agent operates inside a dedicated, funded account with hard limits you configure, and every action settles on-chain for a transparent, verifiable record.

Note: The app you can launch from this site is a fully client-side simulation. It demonstrates the mechanics safely and does not place real orders or move real funds.

The flywheel loop

Olea runs a single closed loop. Each turn moves a little more of your spare change into productive assets and recycles the returns:

  1. Spend. You use your Robinhood card anywhere, like normal. Each purchase emits a transaction event that Olea listens for.
  2. Round-up. The agent rounds the purchase up to the next dollar and multiplies the difference by your chosen multiplier (up to 5×). A $4.30 coffee at 5× captures $3.50 of spare change.
  3. Invest. Most of the captured change is deployed into a tokenized-stock basket on Robinhood Chain, always buying whichever holding is furthest below its target weight.
  4. Earn yield. A reserve slice is parked in a DeFi pool where it earns yield between buys.
  5. Auto-repay. When your statement comes due, accrued yield is swept from the reserve to pay down the card, closing the loop.

Because the output of each step feeds the next, small, frequent contributions compound over time. That is the flywheel.

Tokenized-stock basket

Your invested change buys into a themed basket of tokenized stocks that settle on Robinhood Chain. The default “AI Megacap” basket targets a weighting across large-cap names, but the concept generalizes to any set of target weights.

  • Fractional by design. Round-ups are small, so every buy is fractional. Tokenization makes sub-dollar positions practical.
  • Drift-correcting. Rather than splitting every tiny deposit across every holding (which wastes fees), the agent directs each round-up to the single most underweight token, steadily pulling the basket back toward its targets.
  • Transparent. Holdings, prices and share counts are visible in the app in real time, and each buy has an on-chain settlement record.

DeFi yield & the reserve

Not every dollar goes straight into stocks. A portion of each round-up is held back as a reserve and deposited into a DeFi pool on Robinhood Chain, where it earns a variable yield. The reserve does two jobs: it smooths out buying, and it builds the pool of yield that later repays your card. Yields are variable and are not guaranteed.

Auto-repay

The reserve is what closes the loop. When there is enough yield-bearing balance above your minimum card buffer, the agent withdraws from the reserve and applies it to your outstanding card balance. The effect is a card that partly pays itself, funded by the returns on money you would otherwise have spent and forgotten.

The agent’s logic

Every round-up runs through an explainable decision function. In plain terms, the agent:

  • Checks your guardrails first. If the daily cap is reached, it holds. If a deployment exceeds the manual-approval threshold, it queues for your explicit OK instead of executing.
  • Splits the round-up into an invest slice and a reserve slice.
  • Chooses the most underweight basket token and buys the invest slice of it.
  • Deposits the reserve slice into the DeFi pool.
  • Records a human-readable reason for every action, so the activity log always explains what happened and why.

Guardrails

Autonomy is bounded by rules you set. The agent can only ever touch funds inside a dedicated account, and within that account it is constrained by:

  • Round-up multiplier: how aggressively spare change is captured.
  • Per-trade cap: the largest single order the agent may place.
  • Daily invest cap: the most that can be deployed in a day.
  • Manual-approval threshold: deployments above this pause for your approval.
  • Minimum card buffer: a floor the reserve is never drained below.
Guardrails reduce risk; they do not remove it. See the Disclosures for the full risk picture.

Architecture

The live demo is a fully client-side application, so it runs on any static host with no backend. The in-browser engine models four things:

  • A market that ticks tokenized-stock prices.
  • A chain layer that holds tokenized positions and accrues DeFi yield on the reserve.
  • An agent that makes the deployment and repayment decisions above.
  • A card feed that generates purchases and computes round-ups.

State lives only in your browser tab. Nothing is sent to a server.

Going live

The codebase keeps clearly-marked seams for wiring the real Robinhood network in place of the simulation:

  • Trading MCP: routes real orders through Robinhood’s Agentic Account, the same guardrailed surface used for agent trading.
  • Robinhood Chain: settlement of tokenized-stock buys and the DeFi reserve.
  • Card webhook: the inbound feed of real card purchases that starts each loop.

Each seam is a single, well-defined boundary, so the rest of the app never needs to know whether it is talking to the simulator or the real broker.
